| Absence of Global Symmetries in Quantum Gravity | `It is believed that quantum gravity admits no global symmetries \cite{vanBeest:2021lhn}. Here a global symmetry is defined by a group $G$ admitting a representation on the quantum gravity Hilbert space that preserves the space of local operators and which commutes with the Hamiltonian, with at least one local operator that is charged under $G$.`<br><br>`Properties of black holes can be used to argue heuristically against the existence of global symmetries. Assume one could create black holes of arbitrary global charge. On the one hand, the no-hair theorem implies that one cannot measure their charge, and hence such black holes would have infinite entropy, in contradiction with the Bekenstein-Hawking formula \cite{Bekenstein:1973ur,Hawking:1975sw}. On the other hand, if the black hole evaporates ([Hawking radiation](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Hawking_radiation.qmd)) and all radiation is neutral under the global symmetry \cite{Hawking:1975sw}, one is left with long-lived remnants carrying arbitrary charges. Such an infinite tower of remnants would give an infinite density of states at low energies, leading to loss of predictivity in scattering processes or destabilization of the vacuum \cite{Preskill:1992tc,Banks:2010zn}.`<br><br>`Sharper statements can be made in a [holographic](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Global_symmetries_in_holography.qmd) \cite{Harlow:2018tng} or [string theory](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Symmetries_in_string_theory.qmd) \cite{Banks:2010zn} context.` |

| Covariant Surface Charges and Boundary Terms | `In gravity, the main gauge symmetries are diffeomorphisms. On manifolds with boundary, the generators of gauge transformations may contain boundary terms, which lead to non-trivial transformation properties of physical observables \cite{Iyer:1994ys}. The diffeomorphisms that do not leave all physical states and observables invariant give rise to covariant surface charges \cite{REGGE1974286,Iyer:1994ys,Arnowitt:1962hi,Barnich:2001jy}, which act non-trivially on physical states. The asymptotic symmetry group is the quotient of all gauge symmetries by the "trivial" ones, i.e. those that leave all physical states and observables invariant \cite{REGGE1974286}.`<br><br>`The role of boundary terms in Noether's theorems and gauge symmetries is reviewed in \cite{Banados:2016zi}. Note that the notion of "trivial" gauge symmetry is not unique: sometimes gauge symmetries are called trivial if the constraint vanishes on-shell \cite{Henneaux:2010vx}.` |

| Global Spacetime Symmetries and Isometries | `The global component of the asymptotic symmetry group is generated by Killing vector fields, i.e. rigid isometries of the background spacetime \cite{Wald:1984rg}. Examples are the Poincaré, Anti-de-Sitter and de-Sitter symmetries of the maximally symmetric spacetimes of respectively vanishing, negative and positive cosmological constant, in any number of dimensions.`<br><br>`In general, solutions to the Einstein equations may only admit isometries in the neighborhood of a point or of a hypersurface, such as a boundary, a horizon or an initial data surface.`<br><br>`The global component of a gauge group such as the group of spacetime diffeomorphisms does not qualify as a global symmetry in the sense used in quantum gravity, since there exist no local, gauge invariant operators charged under it.` |
| Global Symmetries in Holography | `That asymptotic/gauge symmetries of a gravitational theory are dual to global symmetries of a conformal field theory in a lower number of dimensions is one of the fundamental properties of [holographic dualities](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Holography.qmd) \cite{Aharony:1999ti}.`<br><br>`In AdS/CFT the definition of a global symmetry can be refined to trade the elusive notion of the bulk quantum gravity Hilbert space for the Hilbert space of the dual conformal field theory -- see def. 4.2 of \cite{Harlow:2018tng}. As a result, bulk global symmetries also act as boundary global symmetries, and one can arrange for the bulk operators charged under these symmetries to lead to an inconsistency with entanglement wedge reconstruction \cite{Dong:2016eik}. Further arguments along these lines can be found in Section 4 of \cite{Harlow:2018tng}.` |
