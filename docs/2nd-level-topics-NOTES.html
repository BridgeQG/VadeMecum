    Absence of Global Symmetries in Quantum Gravity

    It is believed that quantum gravity admits no global symmetries \cite{vanBeest:2021lhn}. Here a global symmetry is defined by a group $G$ admitting a representation on the quantum gravity Hilbert space that preserves the space of local operators and which commutes with the Hamiltonian, with at least one local operator that is charged under $G$.

    Properties of black holes can be used to argue heuristically against the existence of global symmetries. Assume one could create black holes of arbitrary global charge. On the one hand, the no-hair theorem implies that one cannot measure their charge, and hence such black holes would have infinite entropy, in contradiction with the Bekenstein-Hawking formula \cite{Bekenstein:1973ur,Hawking:1975sw}. On the other hand, if the black hole evaporates ([Hawking radiation](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Hawking_radiation.qmd)) and all radiation is neutral under the global symmetry \cite{Hawking:1975sw}, one is left with long-lived remnants carrying arbitrary charges. Such an infinite tower of remnants would give an infinite density of states at low energies, leading to loss of predictivity in scattering processes or destabilization of the vacuum \cite{Preskill:1992tc,Banks:2010zn}.

    Sharper statements can be made in a [holographic](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Global_symmetries_in_holography.qmd) \cite{Harlow:2018tng} or [string theory](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Symmetries_in_string_theory.qmd) \cite{Banks:2010zn} context.

    Asymptotic Symmetries in AdS and dS

    Depending on the choice of boundary conditions, the asymptotic symmetry group may be enhanced, in the sense that local diffeomorphisms are also promoted to non-trivial symmetries. Examples are 2 and 3-dimensional asymptotically AdS and dS spacetimes \cite{BrownHenneaux1986,Maldacena:2016upp,Cotler:2019nbi}. In these cases the asymptotic symmetry algebra contains the Virasoro algebra.

    The same algebras sometimes generate the global symmetries of quantum field theories \cite{Zamolodchikov:1989hfa}. Asymptotic symmetries and charges in de Sitter space are also discussed in \cite{Anninos:2010zf}.

    Black Hole Information-Loss Problem

    Semiclassical quantum field theory on a black-hole background predicts [Hawking radiation](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Hawking_radiation.qmd) and evaporation \cite{Hawking:1975vcx}. This sharpens the information-loss paradox and motivates genuine quantum-gravity models.

    Related questions are whether primordial-black-hole remnants store the missing information, and whether infrared structures such as [soft hair](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Soft_hair_and_gravitational_memory.qmd) play a role.

    Black Hole Shadows and Photon Spheres

    Electromagnetic observations probe multiple photon spheres and the shadow of a black hole. Linked eikonal correspondences among [quasinormal modes](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Quasinormal_modes_and_ringdown.qmd), shadows and [greybody factors](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Greybody_factors.qmd) can help distinguish true horizons from ultra-compact mimickers with surfaces just above the would-be horizon.

    BMS Algebra and Asymptotically Flat Spacetimes

    In asymptotically flat spacetimes \cite{Bondi:1962px,Sachs:1962zza} the asymptotic symmetry algebra contains (at least) an angle-dependent enhancement of the Poincaré translation generators, known as the BMS algebra. This is an example of an enhancement of the asymptotic symmetry group, in which local diffeomorphisms are promoted to non-trivial symmetries.

    Corrections to Black Hole Thermodynamics

    Planck-scale deformations of particle creation near a black hole can be parameterized phenomenologically, explicitly including the [Generalized Uncertainty Principle](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/GUP.qmd) (GUP).

    They induce corrections to the temperature, to the entropy (including Rényi and Tsallis generalizations), to the heat capacity and phase structure, to stability, and to the endgame of the evaporation.

    Covariant Surface Charges and Boundary Terms

    In gravity, the main gauge symmetries are diffeomorphisms. On manifolds with boundary, the generators of gauge transformations may contain boundary terms, which lead to non-trivial transformation properties of physical observables \cite{Iyer:1994ys}. The diffeomorphisms that do not leave all physical states and observables invariant give rise to covariant surface charges \cite{REGGE1974286,Iyer:1994ys,Arnowitt:1962hi,Barnich:2001jy}, which act non-trivially on physical states. The asymptotic symmetry group is the quotient of all gauge symmetries by the "trivial" ones, i.e. those that leave all physical states and observables invariant \cite{REGGE1974286}.

    The role of boundary terms in Noether's theorems and gauge symmetries is reviewed in \cite{Banados:2016zi}. Note that the notion of "trivial" gauge symmetry is not unique: sometimes gauge symmetries are called trivial if the constraint vanishes on-shell \cite{Henneaux:2010vx}.

    Entanglement Entropy and the Area Law

    Entanglement across the horizon obeys an area law, suggesting an entanglement origin for black-hole entropy \cite{Bekenstein:1973ur}. This underpins [holography](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Holography.qmd) and emergent spacetime via the Ryu--Takayanagi prescription \cite{Ryu:2006bv} and ER=EPR.

    It also implicates scrambling and complexity, and the tension between the no-hair property and the huge entropy of black holes. AdS/CFT \cite{Maldacena:1997re} is rigorously established only in (2+1) dimensions, and its (3+1)D status is uncertain.

    Global Spacetime Symmetries and Isometries

    The global component of the asymptotic symmetry group is generated by Killing vector fields, i.e. rigid isometries of the background spacetime \cite{Wald:1984rg}. Examples are the Poincaré, Anti-de-Sitter and de-Sitter symmetries of the maximally symmetric spacetimes of respectively vanishing, negative and positive cosmological constant, in any number of dimensions.

    In general, solutions to the Einstein equations may only admit isometries in the neighborhood of a point or of a hypersurface, such as a boundary, a horizon or an initial data surface.

    The global component of a gauge group such as the group of spacetime diffeomorphisms does not qualify as a global symmetry in the sense used in quantum gravity, since there exist no local, gauge invariant operators charged under it.

    Global Symmetries in Holography

    That asymptotic/gauge symmetries of a gravitational theory are dual to global symmetries of a conformal field theory in a lower number of dimensions is one of the fundamental properties of [holographic dualities](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Holography.qmd) \cite{Aharony:1999ti}.

    In AdS/CFT the definition of a global symmetry can be refined to trade the elusive notion of the bulk quantum gravity Hilbert space for the Hilbert space of the dual conformal field theory -- see def. 4.2 of \cite{Harlow:2018tng}. As a result, bulk global symmetries also act as boundary global symmetries, and one can arrange for the bulk operators charged under these symmetries to lead to an inconsistency with entanglement wedge reconstruction \cite{Dong:2016eik}. Further arguments along these lines can be found in Section 4 of \cite{Harlow:2018tng}.

    Greybody Factors

    Greybody factors describe non-thermal distortions of the Hawking radiation. Through eikonal correspondences they are linked to [quasinormal modes](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Quasinormal_modes_and_ringdown.qmd) and to [black-hole shadows](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Black_hole_shadows_and_photon_spheres.qmd), which can help distinguish true horizons from ultra-compact mimickers.

    Higher-form, Discrete and Approximate Global Symmetries

    The arguments against global symmetries in quantum gravity can be refined to also rule out higher-form symmetries and discrete symmetries, but not approximate global symmetries in an effective field theory of gravity \cite{Banks:2010zn,Gaiotto:2014kfa}.

    Higher-form symmetries are an example of the generalized global symmetries introduced in \cite{Gaiotto:2014kfa}.

    Loop Quantum Gravity Dressed-metric and Rainbow Models of Black Holes

    In loop-quantum-gravity dressed-metric and rainbow models, backreaction yields mode-dependent effective spacetimes. Possible signatures are chromatic lensing, Lorentz-invariance violations and mode-dependent Einstein angles.

    Noncommutative Geometry and Black Holes

    Noncommutative geometry is a candidate framework for quantum black holes. It introduces a minimal length, which shifts the Regge--Wheeler potential and the [quasinormal modes](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Quasinormal_modes_and_ringdown.qmd), with linear stability.

    Nonlocal and Fractional Non-Markovian Dynamics

    Nonlocal, fractional, non-Markovian dynamics (for example models using Caputo derivatives) are among the infrared probes and effective descriptions considered for black holes, complementary to [soft hair and gravitational memory](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Soft_hair_and_gravitational_memory.qmd).

    Quasinormal Modes, Ringdown and Echoes

    Gravitational-wave and electromagnetic probes test the quasinormal modes (QNMs) and ringdown of black holes, and possible gravitational-wave echoes.

    Eikonal correspondences link QNMs, [shadows](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Black_hole_shadows_and_photon_spheres.qmd) and [greybody factors](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Greybody_factors.qmd). In [noncommutative geometry](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Noncommutative_geometry_and_black_holes.qmd), the Regge--Wheeler potential and the QNMs are shifted.

    Singularity Resolution

    The resolution of classical singularities is a key target of quantum-gravity models of black holes. The question is tied to the fate of infalling matter, to the status of the [equivalence principle](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Quantum_tests_of_the_equivalence_principle.qmd), and to possible [quantum superpositions of macroscopic geometries](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Quantum_superposition_of_geometries.qmd) or [causal structures](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Quantum_causality.qmd).

    Soft Hair and Gravitational Memory

    [BMS symmetries](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/BMS_algebra.qmd), soft hair and gravitational memory, which are tied to soft-graviton theorems, provide infrared probes related to the black-hole information problem \cite{Strominger:2017zoo}. See also [nonlocal fractional dynamics](/1st-level-topics/2nd-level-topics/Nonlocal_fractional_dynamics.qmd) as another infrared description.

    Symmetries in String Theory

    In perturbative string theory, global symmetries on the string worldsheet give rise to gauge symmetries in the target space. The simplest example is bosonic string theory, where conformal invariance of the worldsheet is generated by Noether charges constructed from currents of definite weights, and leads to vertex operators that create massless gauge vectors in target space \cite{Polchinski:1998rq}.

    Sharper arguments against global symmetries in quantum gravity can be formulated in a string theory context \cite{Banks:2010zn}.

    Weak Gravity and Weak Cosmic Censorship Conjectures

    Consistency tests involving the Weak Gravity and Weak Cosmic Censorship conjectures, together with astrophysical environments such as dark-matter halos, further constrain viable quantum-gravity models of black holes.
